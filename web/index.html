MicroCluster

It detects the signal without identifying the reporter, and won't name a group too small or too covered to name safely.

An illness spreads through a simulated dorm floor. A detector that sees only anonymous symptom reports, never a name or a room, tries to find the outbreak it was never told about. When it finds one, it names the finest scope that is still safe to name, never one small enough to identify a reporter. Everything on this page is simulated.

How it works
  1. Anonymous reports flow in: one symptom category from a fixed checklist, a rough onset, and which group the reporter is in. No name, no free text, no room number.
  2. The detector flags a statistical excess of reports inside a rolling time window, either against a location's neighbours or against a background rate.
  3. Two independent gates then decide whether anything may be said. The statistical gate needs more reports before it will name a larger population; the privacy gate refuses to name a population that is small or almost fully covered by reports. Both gates must pass.
  4. The system names the finest scope that clears both gates. If no scope clears them, it discloses nothing.
