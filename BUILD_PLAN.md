### Agent-based transmission simulator with privacy-preserving detection and a learned-vs-authored benchmark

**Locked:** September 2, 2026. No concept evaluation before October 6.
**Deadline:** October 6, 2026, 11:45 PM EDT

**Builder:** solo · VS Code + Claude Code + GitHub · $0 budget

---

## 1. WHAT THIS IS

A browser-based sandbox that simulates how an illness moves through a small shared
environment (a dorm floor of 20 to 200 people), runs a privacy-preserving detection
engine inside that simulated world, and reports how well the detector recovers the
outbreak it was not told about.

Three things a judge sees:

1. **The animation.** A floor plan of circles. One turns amber. Over simulated days,
   contact through shared spaces spreads it. The detector, watching only anonymous
   symptom reports, eventually fires.
2. **The delay curve.** Run the simulation hundreds of times, vary the detection
   threshold, and plot detection delay against total infections. This is a result,
   not a screenshot.
3. **The benchmark.** A classifier trained on the simulator's own ground truth,
   scored head to head against the hand-authored threshold rules, in a table.

The thesis sentence: **it detects the signal without exposing the person who created it.**

---

## 2. WHY THIS SHAPE

The detection engine already exists and is tested (37 tests). What it lacked was a
world to run in and users to report into it. A simulator supplies both without
pretending: synthetic data in a simulator is not a workaround, it is the point.

This also converts the project's weakest property into a neutral one. A deployed
reporting tool with zero users has to explain why nobody uses it. A simulator does not.

## 3. HONEST FLAWS

**3.1 Circularity in the ML benchmark — the sharpest criticism available.**
A classifier trained on data generated by your own assumptions will perform well on
data from those same assumptions. It learns the simulator, not epidemiology. Do not
claim otherwise.
*Mitigation:* state the claim narrowly ("which detector better recovers ground truth
under these stated assumptions"), and test robustness by training on one parameter
set and evaluating on a different one. If the learned model only wins on its training
distribution, report that. A negative result honestly reported scores better than an
inflated positive one.

**3.2 No real-world validation.** Contact rates, transmission probability and
reporting rate are chosen, not measured. The model predicts nothing about any real
building.
*Mitigation:* a permanent on-screen statement, not a footnote. Cite the parameter
sources you did use.

**3.3 Indirect health impact.** Nobody is treated, diagnosed or protected by this. The
impact claim is understanding, not intervention.
*Mitigation:* make the claim you can defend. A student who watches one case become
fourteen understands why last winter's floor-wide cold moved the way it did.

**3.4 Scope risk.** Simulator, detector integration, ML benchmark, API, frontend,
video and PDF in 34 solo days is achievable only with a plain visual layer and a hard
freeze.

**3.5 Prior art.** Agent-based epidemic models are a mature field, small-cell
suppression is standard practice in public health data publishing, and neither is
yours. Cite both. Your contribution is the combination and the deployment context.

## 9. RULES THAT DO NOT CHANGE

- Fixed symptom checklist. Never free text. No natural-language interpretation anywhere.
- Clustering at category level only. The system never infers a diagnosis.
- Two detectors in parallel: relative (versus contemporaneous peers) and absolute (versus background rate).
- Two disclosure gates, both must pass: statistical (scales up with n) and privacy (scales down with n).
- Disclose the finest scope passing both. Never a hardcoded tier list.
- Privacy evaluation after detection, before presentation. Raw reports never reach the client.
- Every threshold is a named, configurable constant with a comment. They are project policies, not proven laws.
- Keep both the authored rules and the learned model in the final product. The model never silently replaces the rules.
